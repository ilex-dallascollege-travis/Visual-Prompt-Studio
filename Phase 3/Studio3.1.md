Prompt 1

Historical theme: Art and the afterlife
 Historical period: Ancient Egypt, Old Kingdom (c. 2580 to 2510 BCE)
 Artwork used as inspiration: Pyramids at Giza (3.1.13)
 
Create an image of a monumental architectural structure, an Old Kingdom Egyptian pyramid tomb from ancient Egypt, c. 2500 BCE, shown at sunrise on the Giza plateau.

The pyramid is built of massive, carefully stacked stone blocks clad in smooth white limestone, with a gold-tipped peak representing the sun god Re. Its strong diagonal lines meet at a single apex, forming a pure, symmetrical triangle with a stable, balanced composition. Each side is aligned to a cardinal direction. The texture contrasts the rough, layered stone of the blocks with the smooth, polished limestone casing and the glowing gold capstone. The pyramid is the clear focal point and dominates the flat, open desert space around it. Its enormous scale is emphasized by tiny figures of workers and priests near the base. A small funerary temple sits on the east side, facing the rising sun. The palette is warm sand, ochre, and white against a deep blue sky, with long shadows to show depth.

This pyramid was built because Egyptians believed their pharaohs ruled with the authority of the gods, and the tomb protected the pharaoh's ka (life force) so he could be reborn in the afterlife. The east-facing temple reflects the belief that, like the rising sun, the pharaoh would be reborn. In a society organized around divine kingship and belief in eternity, devotion to the pharaoh and faith in the afterlife pushed people to build it.

Prompt 2

Historical theme: Art as devotional labor and sacred text
 Historical period: Middle Ages, Islamic Spain and the Maghreb (late 13th century)
 Artwork used as inspiration: Page from the Koran (3.2.10)
 
Create an image of an illuminated manuscript page, a hand-copied Koran page from the late 13th century Islamic Middle Ages, made in Spain and the Maghreb. The medium is ink and gold on vellum.

Lines of elegant Arabic calligraphy run from right to left across a cream-colored vellum page, written in dark ink in the regional Maghribi script. The headings use the older, bolder Kufic script, and the thicker lettering in gold marks the start of a new chapter (surah). A large circular gold ornament filled with swirling arabesque patterns of intertwined geometric and plant forms divides the text into sections. The texture contrasts the slightly rough, aged surface of the vellum with the raised, shimmering gold leaf. The composition is balanced and rhythmic, with repeated horizontal lines of text, and the gold headings and ornament create emphasis against the dark ink. The blank margins give the page a calm, contemplative sense of space. There are no human figures and no image of God, so line, pattern, and calligraphy carry all the beauty.

This page was made because, in the medieval Islamic world, the word of Allah was the sacred focus of art, and images of Allah were never shown. Making a manuscript by hand, from preparing the vellum to careful copying and gold decoration, took great effort that was considered a tribute to God. In a society where devotion was expressed through script and pattern instead of images, the Koran page turned the sacred text itself into art.
