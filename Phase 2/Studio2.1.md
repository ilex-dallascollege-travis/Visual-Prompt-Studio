Ilex Travis 


Just Keep Swimmin'

Prompt A

An oil painting on canvas of a sea turtle seen in profile at mid-depth, gliding right, away from the viewer through a coral reef. A single light source from the surface above illuminates the entire scene. Render the turtle using chiaroscuro where the highlight is the top left of the turtles shell/ its shell. With the light reflecting off glistening through the water light prisms. With shadows casted beneath the turtle the water is darker below as you can tell the sun lightening down. The shadow will continue to cast into the depths. A core shadow along the lower right edge of the shell, where it curves away from the light.  Build the water in thin transparent glazes so that light reflecting between the layers creates luminosity and a sense of depth. Paint the coral in thick impasto applied with a palette knife and filbert brush, so the ridges have a three-dimensional presence and an implied texture you could feel with your hand. Blend the water and soften edges with a fan brush. Distribute the full range of the color wheel across the reef - yellow sun coral,  a floor of red and pink sea fans with tiny blueish silver minnows with shadows casting from the minnow schools, the casted shadows on the coral below them , orange and white shrimps crawling around in a couple small groups across the organ pipe coral; it is violet/blue/white, blueish green brain coral with crabs tucked off in the crevices a colony of crabs live in the brain coral section. A top section of blue where there are a couple of starfishes on them, and violet encrusting coral walking off the edge into the depths with oysters and sea barnacles attached in a couple of spots. There is also leafy cup coral mixed throughout the sections, along with moss and seaweed rising from the depths. Along with mixed in components there are small clams through the reef, only three of the 5 throughout the artwork has one pearl that reflects light off into the piece as a light prism. The oil's slow drying time allows the glazes to be blended and layered over multiple sessions. In the tradition of oil glazing pioneered by Northern Renaissance painters such as Jan van Eyck, who in the fifteenth century mastered thin glazes that let light reflect between layers to create luminosity, unify the whole composition beneath a single transparent blue-green glaze, so every hue is seen through the same water. Highlight the theme of sonder. The realization that every other creature's existence is as complex as one's own, by giving equal painterly attention to every fish, anemone, and shell in the reef, so that no single figure, not even the turtle, is permitted to dominate the composition. Recede into space by rendering distant coral cooler, lighter, and softer-edged than the foreground.



Prompt B

A monotype print of a sea turtle seen in profile at mid-depth gliding left, away from the viewer through a coral reef. In the tradition of the dark-field monotype technique developed by Edgar Degas in the 1870s–1890s, the plate is fully inked with a brayer and then worked from dark masses toward light by wiping ink away rather than drawing lines, under a single light source from above. The water is built by wiping the ink away in soft strokes where the artwork is closer to sea level, leaving the ink heavy and dark where the artwork grows into the depths, toward the bottomless abyss of the sea. Render the turtle by wiping diagonally from top right to bottom left as the light from the highlight hits it top right of the shell. The rag is then pulled across the shadow of the turtle using chiaroscuro so it looks as if the shadow is a different color. Leaving the ink heavy and untouched along the lower right edge where it curves into shadow. The coral is not built up but scraped, dragged, scratched through the wet ink, or incised with a stick or brush handle while the ink is still wet into the inked plate, so texture comes from the roughness of the removed ink rather than raised paint. I want to simplify the cast since a single gestural pull cant hold as much incidental detail as built-up oil. The palette colors are going to be a black ink as the dominant tone, with a single blue-green accent for the water, and the warm color reserved only for the pearls. Highlight the theme of sonder. The realization that every other creature's existence is as complex as one's own, by giving equal painterly attention to every fish, anemone, and shell in the reef, so that no single figure, not even the turtle, is permitted to dominate the composition. Distant coral fades into looser, lighter, less-worked passages of ink toward the depths.
